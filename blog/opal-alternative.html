Looking for an Opal Alternative? 3 Options for iPhone

By Carlos Velasquez, founder of Pause · October 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Opal is one of the most popular screen-time apps on iPhone, and for good reason: it can lock you out of distracting apps so firmly that there's no way back in. But a hard wall isn't what everyone needs. If Opal feels like too much, too expensive, or just not your style, here are three alternatives worth a look.

Full disclosure: I make Pause, one of the apps below, so weigh my take accordingly. Facts and prices come from each company's website, Apple Support, and the US App Store. Last checked: October 19, 2026.

What Opal does well

Opal is a full blocking toolkit. You can block apps and websites on a schedule, set daily limits, run a focus timer, and track your time as a daily Opal Score. Its strictest setting can't be turned off once it starts, which is exactly what some people need. It runs on iPhone, Mac, and Android, and the iPhone app requires iOS 18 or later. It's free to download, with weekly, monthly, and yearly plans listed on the App Store (the yearly plan is listed at $99.99).

People usually look for an alternative for one of three reasons: they want something free, they find hard blocks stressful or end up deleting the app, or they don't want to give up their apps, just the mindless opening of them.

1. Apple Screen Time (free, built in)

Screen Time is already on your iPhone. In iOS 27, you set Time Allowances for categories like Social Media and Games, and Screen Time Schedules for when those apps are available. Always Allowed keeps the apps you need open at all times. On iOS 26 and earlier, the same tools are called App Limits and Downtime, and Apple notes that limits can be ignored once you reach them unless you turn on blocking with a Screen Time passcode.

Best for: seeing where your time goes and setting gentle limits for free. Trade-off: a limit you set for yourself is easy to lift yourself.

2. Forest (focus sessions with a reward)

Forest turns focus into a game: start a timer and a virtual tree grows while you stay off your phone. Its Allow List blocks distracting apps during a session, and Time Guard blocks apps on a schedule (Forest says the free version covers one app or site). Forest also says it has funded over 2 million real trees through the nonprofit Trees for the Future. It's free to download with optional Forest Plus subscriptions, and it runs on iPhone, iPad, Android, and Chrome and Firefox.

Best for: study and deep-work sessions. Trade-off: it's built around sessions you start on purpose, so it helps less with the random, all-day checking.

3. Pause (a quick puzzle instead of a wall)

Pause takes a middle path between limits you can ignore and walls you can't get through. You choose the apps, categories, or websites that pull you in. When you open one, Pause asks you to solve a short puzzle first, like Word Jumble, Five Letters, or Ball Sort. Then you pick how long to stay: 5, 10, 15, or 20 minutes. When the time is up, the pause comes back on its own.

That few-second puzzle is often enough to notice the reflex and decide whether you actually want to go in. You can also set named free windows, like lunch, when chosen apps open without a puzzle, and see how many times you walked away. Pause is built on Apple's Screen Time framework, is free to download, and offers a 7-day free trial of Premium ($4.99 a month or $49.99 a year).

Best for: people who want to keep their apps but stop opening them on autopilot. Trade-off: it's iPhone only, and there's no lock-you-out mode by design. If you truly need a wall you can't get past, Opal is stricter.

Quick comparison

  • Opal: strict blocking on a schedule, works across iPhone, Mac, and Android. Free download, paid plans.
  • Apple Screen Time: free and built in, gentle limits that are easy to lift.
  • Forest: timed focus sessions with a virtual (and real) forest. Free download, optional Plus.
  • Pause: a short puzzle before chosen apps open, then a timed unlock. Free download, 7-day Premium trial.

Which one should you pick?

Ask yourself how you break your own limits. If you blow past them on purpose and regret it, you need a harder wall: stay with Opal, or use Screen Time with a passcode someone else holds. If you open apps without thinking, a small, consistent bit of friction tends to work better than a wall you'll eventually remove. That's the idea behind Pause.
